\title{ArcSettle: Reserve-Gated USDC Settlement with\\ On-Chain Proof-of-Reserves on Arc}
\author{Dipankar Sarkar \\ \small doom2quake}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Fiat-backed stablecoins settle on-chain but prove solvency off-chain. The chain sees
tokens move; it does not see whether those tokens are backed at the instant they are
settled. That gap between reported reserves and reserves proven \emph{right now} is
where depegs and insolvency events live, and it sharpens the moment an autonomous
treasury agent settles or mints on a schedule against a figure it last read. We present
ArcSettle, a settlement rail for Arc, Circle's settlement chain, whose every settlement
is gated on-chain by a signed proof-of-reserves attestation: settled supply provably
cannot exceed the USDC reserves that have actually been proven. A redemption does
\emph{not} reopen settlement headroom until a new attestation has observed the outflow,
which closes a redeem-then-resettle loop that naive reserve gates admit. A reserve-aware
operator agent runs the same gate off-chain first, so a doomed settlement never spends
gas. This paper describes the design and the milestone-1 implementation: two
self-contained Solidity contracts and an operator agent, verified by 59 Solidity tests
and 75 Python tests. All work is Arc testnet only; no mainnet, no live broadcast, no
users, and no audit (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
A regulated dollar stablecoin such as USDC~\cite{usdc} holds reserves at a bank and
asserts that circulating supply never exceeds them. The assertion is an off-chain
attestation; the chain enforces nothing about it. Three failure shapes follow. First,
an attestation \emph{lags}: supply is settled against a reserve figure that a bank
outflow has already invalidated. Second, a \emph{redemption} frees mint or settlement
headroom the instant supply falls, before the corresponding reserve outflow is observed,
so an operator can redeem, move the freed collateral out, and re-settle against the same
stale proof. Third, a \emph{stale or future-dated} timestamp is treated as eternally
fresh. Anyone who accepts the token as settlement --- a merchant, a payment processor, a
treasury desk --- wears these gaps and cannot see them until they have already cost
something.

The failure sharpens under an autonomous agent on the treasury. An agent that
rebalances or settles on a schedule will, unless something stops it, submit against
whatever reserve figure it last read, or worse against a figure a redemption has already
invalidated. ``We never settle past proven reserves'' has to be an on-chain invariant
the agent cannot argue its way around, not a policy in a runbook.

\section{Why Arc}
Arc is Circle's own settlement chain, with USDC as native gas and value and first-class
access to Circle's settlement primitives (CCTP~\cite{cctp}, Wallets,
Gateway)~\cite{circlearc}. That makes it the natural home for a proof-of-reserves
settlement rail: the reserve asset, the settled asset, and the gas asset are the same
regulated dollar, so the invariant ``circulating never exceeds attested reserves'' is
expressed in one unit end to end, with no bridge-risk seam in the middle of the
guarantee. On a general-purpose chain this product is a bolt-on; on Arc it is native to
how value already moves. Because balances track USDC's native six decimals, no
unit-conversion step sits between the proof and the gate.

\section{Design}
ArcSettle is two self-contained contracts and an operator agent. The invariant is:
\[
\text{totalSupply} + \text{amount} \;\le\; \text{effectiveReserves},
\]
checked on every settlement, where
\[
\text{effectiveReserves} =
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if the attestation is stale or future-dated} \\
\max(0,\; R - D) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
$R$ is the attested USDC reserve figure, and $D$ is redemption debt: USDC redeemed that
the current attestation has not yet accounted for.

\subsection{The proof path: \texttt{ReserveAttestationOracle}}
Reserve figures do not enter the oracle because a privileged account says so. They enter
because someone presents an EIP-712~\cite{eip712} signature from a registered attestor
over $(\text{reserves}, \text{attestedAt}, \text{nonce})$. The contract recovers the
signer on-chain and refuses: unknown signers; replays (a strictly incrementing nonce);
malleable signatures (low-$s$ check, cf.\ EIP-2098~\cite{eip2098}); wrong-length or
bad-$v$ signatures; and backdated or future-dated attestations. Submission is
permissionless --- the signature, not the caller, is the authority --- so liveness never
depends on one relayer's key. The attestor set is the only trust root, and it is governed
asymmetrically: \emph{adding} an attestor (the direction that could prove reserves out of
nothing) is a two-day timelocked, publicly announced change; \emph{removing} one (which
can only shrink what the oracle believes) is immediate, so a leaked key is revocable in
the block it is discovered. The digest is bound to the chain id and the contract address,
so an attestation signed for one deployment cannot be replayed against another.

\subsection{The gate: \texttt{ArcSettlement}}
\texttt{ArcSettlement} is a minimal, correct ERC-20~\cite{eip20} settlement-claim token
(six decimals, checked arithmetic, custom errors, events on every state change) whose
\texttt{settle} function reverts with \texttt{InsufficientReserves},
\texttt{StaleAttestation}, or \texttt{FutureAttestation} as appropriate. Three properties
are load-bearing:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{The operator cannot conjure headroom.} The oracle and the freshness bound
change only through a two-step, two-day-timelocked proposal that is public the moment it
is proposed, and \texttt{maxAttestationAge} is clamped to $[1\text{ min}, 7\text{ days}]$,
so ``turn the freshness check off'' is not a reachable state.
\item \textbf{Redemption does not silently reopen headroom.} Redeeming frees supply but
the freed amount is booked as \texttt{pendingRedemptions} and subtracted from attested
reserves until a \emph{new} attestation (higher \texttt{attestationId}) lands that has
observed the outflow. This is the defence against the redeem-move-collateral-resettle
attack.
\item \textbf{Time is handled defensively.} A future-dated timestamp reverts instead of
reading as eternally fresh, and freshness is computed as
\texttt{block.timestamp - attestedAt} so no addition can overflow.
\end{enumerate}

Every \texttt{IReserveOracle} getter is \texttt{view}, so the gate reads the oracle with
\texttt{STATICCALL}; a malicious oracle that tries to re-enter \texttt{settle} while being
read cannot change state, and the settlement fails closed.

\subsection{The operator agent}
The agent (\texttt{arcsettle/}) mirrors the on-chain gate off-chain and refuses a
settlement that would exceed proven reserves rather than broadcasting a doomed
transaction. Ordering is deliberate: an amount outside the \texttt{uint256} domain is
rejected first; then the reserve pre-check; and only a settlement that will actually be
submitted consumes a slot from an action limiter, so a burst of bad proposals cannot lock
out a good one. Every decision is written to an audit trail. In live mode the pre-check is
a real \texttt{eth\_call} dry-run whose revert reason is the contract's own custom error,
not a local guess.

\section{Implementation and evaluation}
The contracts target solc 0.8.24 and are self-contained (no external Solidity library),
so the security surface is auditable in two files. The agent has no third-party runtime
dependency: Keccak-256~\cite{keccak}, selectors, and ABI word codecs are implemented in
about a hundred lines and pinned to published test vectors, so ``live mode'' can never
degrade into an import error.

We evaluate by test, using Foundry~\cite{foundry} for the contracts:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{59 Solidity tests} (\texttt{forge test}): 36 on the settlement gate (the
reserve invariant, freshness, redemption accounting, timelocked oracle rotation,
re-entrancy, ERC-20 plumbing, and a supply-versus-reserves fuzz test) and 23 on the
oracle (EIP-712 recovery, replay, malleability, backdating, and the timelocked attestor
set). Each defence has a test that goes red when the defence is removed.
\item \textbf{75 Python tests} (\texttt{pytest}): the operator agent against an offline
model of the contract, the dependency-free ABI layer pinned to solc's own selectors, and
live-mode configuration validation and revert decoding against a fake node. Many carry a
named Solidity counterpart, so the two suites read side by side.
\end{itemize}

The redeem-then-resettle defence is the sharpest test:
\texttt{test\_RedeemThenResettleAgainstOldAttestationReverts} settles $10^6$ USDC against
$10^6$ reserves, redeems $4\times10^5$, restates the same proof (no new id) as if the USDC
had been moved out, and asserts the re-settle reverts, leaving supply at $6\times10^5$.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
This is milestone 1, and the honest scope is narrow.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{No mainnet}, and none planned under this grant. All work is Arc testnet only;
the contract is not deployed anywhere yet (testnet deployment is milestone 2).
\item \textbf{No live broadcast.} Live mode is read-only: reads and settle dry-runs are
real \texttt{eth\_call}s, but broadcasting a signed settlement needs a funded testnet key
and a signer this repo does not ship. That path, with a Circle-primitive custody seam, is
milestone 3.
\item \textbf{No users, no revenue, no audit, no partnership with Circle.} The substitute
for traction is mutation-tested code and a candid scope map, not usage numbers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
ArcSettle makes ``an agent provably cannot settle past proven reserves'' an on-chain
invariant on Arc, denominated end to end in USDC, with a redemption defence that naive
reserve gates lack and an operator agent that never burns gas on a settlement the chain
would reject. Milestone 1 delivers the contract and the proof path, tested. The durable
contribution is a standard, tested answer to how an agent settles in USDC on Arc without
ever exceeding proven reserves, established before mainnet.
